# Lusca

> The Lusca is a marine cryptid of Bahamian and Caribbean folklore, said to inhabit the blue holes of Andros Island and surrounding waters. It is most often described as a gigantic octopus or a half-octopus, half-shark or half-dragon hybrid, and is blamed locally for the deaths of swimmers, divers and small craft drawn into blue-hole tidal currents.

## Overview

The Lusca is a marine cryptid reported from the Caribbean, primarily around the blue holes of Andros Island in The Bahamas. The name appears to be a clipped form of Mollusca, the phylum of cephalopods, reflecting the creature's tentacled aspect; folklore variously describes it as a gigantic octopus, a giant cuttlefish or a hybrid of octopus and shark, sometimes octopus and dragon. Reported sizes are exaggerated — up to and beyond 23 metres (75 feet) — well exceeding any documented octopus species. The creature's habitat is closely tied to blue holes: vertical underwater sinkholes and cave systems common around Andros (which has more than 170 inland and 50 oceanic blue holes) that are 100–120 metres deep on average and produce powerful tidal currents and whirlpools as the tide changes. Local tradition explains drownings, lost dinghies and missing divers around blue holes as the work of the Lusca. The Canadian wildlife biologist Bruce S. Wright was among the first researchers to document the legend, after a guide on Andros described a 'half-octopus, half-dragon' creature with sucker-tipped tentacles inhabiting limestone caverns beneath an inland blue hole; Wright suggested a giant octopus or rare giant squid. The French cryptozoologist Michel Raynal has proposed that the Lusca might represent an unusually large cirrate octopus, noting a 1984 French submersible (Cyana) record of an undescribed Pacific cirrate octopus measuring about 2.5 m. Television journalist Jeremy Wade investigated the Lusca for River Monsters ('Terror in Paradise', S8E4), concluding that a large octopus is the most plausible candidate behind the legend.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Giant octopus / giant squid** _(Bruce S. Wright, Jeremy Wade)_

The Lusca represents an unusually large octopus or, less commonly, a giant squid (Architeuthis), with reported sizes exaggerated by folklore.

**Giant cirrate octopus** _(Michel Raynal)_

French cryptozoologist Michel Raynal has proposed that the Lusca might represent an unusually large cirrate octopus, citing the 1984 Cyana submersible record of an undescribed Pacific cirrate octopus around 2.5 m.

**Blue-hole hydrology, not a creature** _(Marine geologists and divers)_

Many Lusca-attributed events — drownings, lost boats, sudden whirlpools — are produced by the genuine tidal currents of blue holes rather than by any animal.

### Skeptical

**Folkloric explanation for hazardous waters** _(Mainstream folklore commentators)_

The Lusca is best understood as a Bahamian folk explanation for the genuine but mundane dangers of blue holes — strong currents, sharks, and isolated drowning incidents — magnified in oral tradition.
